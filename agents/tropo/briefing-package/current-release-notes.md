# Tropo v1.95.0 — Release Notes

*The current Tropo release. Delivered by Tropo's release-liaison role at Pattern 1 returning user startup OR Pattern 3 scheduled summary.*

**Headline: the arrival release. Your studio is born on your machine, and Po meets you there.**

- **Your studio is born on your machine, not in the box.** The box ships no identity of any
  studio, ours included, and the build refuses to seal a box that carries one. The first time you
  open it, genesis mints your studio's identity here: a prefix every new identifier carries, the
  vault entity, your own inbox. Every agent notices when a studio has no identity yet; only Po
  mints; nothing mints silently.
- **Po greets you and asks who you are.** She notices the studio has no name, asks your name and
  the studio's name and purpose, mints through that conversation (your founder principal goes
  through the same governed mint every identifier does), and offers a first agent by name: Cal,
  Darin, or one from scratch. The offer and your answer are events on the bus, so the arrival is
  on the record afterwards.
- **The box proves its own claims before it is sealed.** Every build guard is a registered gate,
  run at two points: on the tree before the word "build", where every failure is reported at once
  with the harm it prevents named in one line, and on the assembled box before the zip closes,
  where every path a shipped instruction tells you to open must exist, the operating principles
  must be complete, the changelog must name the version shipped, and no studio identity may be
  present. This box is the first built through it.
- **The Studio Map and the Architecture Review ship as HTML.** Open `boards/po/studio-map.html`
  first: a visual of the studio in three layers, the subsystems with live counts, the governed
  types, the rules that bind you, and a resources section, every section derived from the box's
  own files at build time. The review ships beside it with its fifteen figures.
- **The studio smoke test ships, and so does memory sovereignty.** One script runs five real
  operations against your studio and catches the failure that costs the most: a gate that fails
  closed on normal work. The shipped operating principles now carry the memory principle, so any
  agent you create keeps its learnings in Tropo memory, never in a harness-private store.

*If you are arriving from v1.93.0: v1.94.0 was never published. Everything it built ships here for
the first time as well: a colleague can join by one owner-signed gesture and two of you can edit the
same governed file through a merge seam with a gate that can still say no; orient answers a typed
question with citations that resolve to your real imported documents; Cal and Darin are in the box;
and the delivery channel behind updates was rebuilt.*

*Stated plainly: four items are carried to v1.96 by name in `KNOWN-LIMITATIONS.md` (a task you mint
may parent to an inbox your studio never has until the mint resolves your own; a full index rebuild
drops hub frontmatter keys that a single-entry rebuild keeps; cutting your own release locks from
`design` until two tools agree on the statuses; whether an entry ships is decided by the ship
manifest, not by the entry's `extraction_scope`). The release's own acceptance walks on the shipped
box had not run when these notes were written; the fire waits on them. This is the record of what is
built; those runs decide whether it works where it is going.*

## Cured after the plan lock, under the freeze rule

**The identity check's fatal floor.** The studio status tool reported a fresh box, one with no
identity yet, as broken; found by the arrival-walk harness on a candidate, cured, and re-walked to
zero findings.

**The smoke test's negative arm.** After a legacy-alias change the test could not see its own
plant; the fixture now removes visibility as intended, re-verified by a non-author.

**The shipped instructions.** 101 references in shipped playbooks named files a box does not carry;
before the first candidate they reached zero by repointing to what ships, retiring to plain words,
declaring files a runbook itself creates, and excluding superseded playbooks and the kernel's
history companions by ship-manifest deny rows. The candidate builder now arms the ship manifest
exactly as the release build does.
