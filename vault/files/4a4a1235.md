## Decision

**Tropo-OS ships under the Apache License, Version 2.0 going forward.**

The canonical LICENSE file at `argo-os/vault/templates/root-docs/LICENSE` (which ships to user Studios via ship-artifact entry `4a7e1c9b`) now contains the full Apache 2.0 license text (OSI canonical form) with copyright notice "Copyright 2026 Tropo AI, Inc."

Effective: 2026-05-18 (v1.41.0 ship — first release under the new license).

## Context

The Tropo-OS LICENSE shipped under GNU Affero General Public License v3 (AGPL-3) by default. The choice was implicit (no prior ADR codified AGPL-3); the AGPL-3 short-form notice was authored at `vault/templates/root-docs/LICENSE` on 2026-04-25 by Mike-Maziarz when the templates infrastructure landed.

Talos T4 was commissioned 2026-05-15 to research Apache 2.0 vs MIT for the Tropo-OS public release (work surfaced as part of Brief #10 "GitHub Releases Integration" Open Decision #3 + the broader tropo-ai.com Launch Foundations project). Talos's deep research at [b7e9c41d](b7e9c41d.md) concluded Apache 2.0.

Mike confirmed the Apache 2.0 direction 2026-05-16. Talos filed `c5d8e421` to Argus's dev-pipeline inbox with the substrate work needed: LICENSE swap + downstream audit + ADR authoring + ship-artifact entry update. The handoff sat across A65 → A70 unexecuted.

Argus A71 absorbed the work into v1.41.0 Stream A per Mike-A71 captain-mode walk 2026-05-18, after Argus's own build-artifact inspection surfaced that v1.40.0 was actively shipping AGPL-3 to every download — making the license swap an active shipping defect rather than future-cycle work.

## Why Apache 2.0 (per Talos's research, summarized)

Talos's research at `b7e9c41d` is the deep-dive; key points for the decision record:

- **Apache 2.0's patent-license grant** (Section 3) protects users from patent claims by contributors. Critical for ecosystem-building.
- **Apache 2.0 is permissive but not bare-permissive.** Requires attribution + change-marking + NOTICE preservation. Stronger than MIT (which is bare-permissive).
- **Apache 2.0 is the de facto enterprise OSS standard.** Lower friction for adoption by companies whose legal teams have already approved Apache 2.0 internally.
- **Apache 2.0 is compatible with the dual licensing model** Tropo may want to adopt later (open-source Apache 2.0 for the OS, separate commercial license for hosted platform). MIT could also accommodate; AGPL-3 actively conflicts (copyleft cascade complicates dual-license).
- **AGPL-3's network-use copyleft trigger** would have required every Studio operating Tropo to license their derivative substrate as AGPL-3 — incompatible with the "every Studio is your own; what you produce in it is yours" framing Mike has been pinning since the founding charter.

The AGPL-3 default would have actively undermined the Tropo product thesis. Apache 2.0 aligns with it.

## What this ADR does NOT cover

- **Pre-v1.41 release re-licensing.** Releases shipped before v1.41.0 (v1.27.0 → v1.40.0) keep their as-shipped AGPL-3 license per OSS hygiene (historical releases preserve original terms). New downloads of v1.41.0+ get Apache 2.0. Per OSS norms, this is the right boundary.
- **The public GitHub repo creation.** `github.com/tropo-ai/tropo-os` doesn't exist yet; Talos T4 flagged it as gated on the license swap landing per Brief #10. This ADR is the gating substrate; repo creation moves from blocked to unblocked. Repo creation itself is Talos-lane.
- **Commercial-license design.** The future commercial-license (for hosted Studios / Tropo platform / marketplace) is separate substrate. Apache 2.0 for the OS doesn't preclude or prescribe the commercial side.

## Consequences

**Positive:**

- Removes the network-copyleft trigger that would have cascaded onto users of hosted Studios.
- Aligns with enterprise OSS adoption norms.
- Compatible with Tropo's future dual-licensing model.
- Closes a 2-day shipping defect (every v1.40 download since 2026-05-17 shipped wrong LICENSE).

**Negative (or trade-offs):**

- Apache 2.0 is permissive — derivative works can re-license under non-Apache terms. AGPL-3 would have enforced copyleft cascade. Trade-off: ecosystem reach over derivative control.
- Patent-license clause (Section 3) creates a defensive-termination trigger if a Tropo user sues a contributor over the Work. Trade-off: standard OSS pattern.
