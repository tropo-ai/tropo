# How the Gardener Works

As a studio grows, some of its knowledge goes stale — a note nobody's touched in months, a draft that got left behind, a document the work moved past. Left unattended, stale content quietly makes the whole graph less trustworthy: your agents read it as if it were still true.

The **gardener** is Tropo's answer. It watches your studio on its own schedule and **flags** what looks stale — so you're reading a graph that tells the truth. And it does exactly that much: it flags. It never decides for you.

## It flags. It never reaps.

This is the load-bearing promise, so it's worth stating plainly:

**The gardener never archives, moves, recycles, or deletes anything.** It annotates — it stamps a "looks stale" marker on Tropo's derived index, the read-only layer Tropo builds *from* your files. Your actual files are never touched by the gardener, not even to record the flag.

Acting on a flag is always a human decision, made through the normal archive and disposition tools. The gardener surfaces candidates; **you reap.** A flagged item keeps its active status until you say otherwise. There is no path where the gardener quietly cleans house.

## Why you see a short list, not half your studio

The raw staleness signals are deliberately over-eager. Turned loose and unfiltered, they'd flag something like *half* the graph — and the great majority of those would be false alarms (a founding document isn't stale just because it's old; an intentionally-preserved record isn't rot).

So the board never shows you the raw wash. It shows the **adjudicated set** that remains after the known false-positive patterns are filtered out — a small slice of what the raw signals first light up. The goal is a list short enough that a human can actually look at it, where most of what's on it genuinely deserves a look.

## How it decides something looks stale

The gardener reads a few **structural signals** from the graph — things like whether anything still points at a record, and whether it's been superseded — plus one **time-based overlay**: how long it's actually been since you touched a private draft, measured on your own clock. That last one catches the case structure alone misses: a draft rotting quietly in a studio that simply hasn't been active.

## The "how old is too old" window is yours to set

The time overlay uses a **decay window** — the number of idle days after which something counts as aged. It ships with sensible defaults, and it's **yours to change, with no code**:

- `decay_threshold_unshared_draft_days` — default **90**. How long an unshared draft can sit untouched before it's aged.
- `decay_threshold_general_days` — default **180**. The same, for general content.

These live as named fields on the **gardener's maintenance-loop entry** in your vault — a `type: loop` file tagged for the gardener. To move the window from 90 days to 120, you open that entry and change the number. The next time the studio rebuilds its index, the gardener uses your new window — no edit to any code, in any studio, by any agent you've entrusted with it.

## Changing the window changes the gardener — and only the gardener

That window is the gardener's alone. If another part of Tropo also pays attention to how old something is — for example, noticing drafts you haven't published yet — it keeps **its own separate threshold**. The two read the same underlying "days since you touched this" reading, but each applies its own cutoff. So turning the gardener's knob to 120 days retunes the gardener and **nothing else** quietly moves with it.

## What this means for you

- **"Does the gardener ever archive or delete on its own?"** No. It only flags; a human always decides whether to act.
- **"Why does the board show a short list instead of half my studio?"** The raw signals over-flag by roughly ten to one. The board shows the adjudicated set that's left after the false alarms are filtered — not the raw wash.
- **"Where's the 'how old is too old' knob, and how do I change it to 120 days?"** On the gardener's maintenance-loop entry in your vault (a `type: loop` file), change `decay_threshold_unshared_draft_days` to `120`. No code. It takes effect on the next index rebuild.
- **"If I change that window, does it move anything else?"** No. Other signals keep their own thresholds; the gardener window governs the gardener alone.

Tropo keeps your knowledge honest by showing you what looks stale — and leaves every decision about what to do with it exactly where it belongs: with you.
