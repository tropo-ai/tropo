# How Import and Export Work

Tropo is built to hold the work you already have — not to make you retype it into a new tool. You drop a real Word document into your studio, it becomes governed content you (and your agents) can read, edit, and version, and when you want it back you export it — with a **receipt that proves nothing was silently lost or mangled** along the way.

This is the same honesty guarantee Tropo already makes when it updates itself — that your work is never silently changed — applied to the boundary where your work comes in and goes back out.

## The round-trip, start to finish

1. **Drop it in.** Put a file into your studio's `04-external-work/` folder — a Word `.docx`, a Markdown `.md`, a PowerPoint `.pptx`, or a PDF. That folder is the doormat: anything you leave there gets noticed.
2. **It becomes governed.** On the next pass, Tropo brings the file in as a governed entry — indexed, addressable, and safe under the same rules as everything else you own. The original file stays right where you left it.
3. **Edit it.** Change the content on disk or in the vault. Your edits are just edits; there's no separate "checked-out" state to manage.
4. **It re-versions itself.** When Tropo next reconciles, your changed work is captured as a new version — the prior version is kept, not overwritten.
5. **Export it back.** Send it back out and Tropo writes a **new version into the original source folder**, alongside the one you started with. Nothing is clobbered; the history is on disk where you can see it.

## Which formats make the full round-trip

Not every format can safely come back out as the same kind of file it went in as, and this release is honest about that line rather than pretending.

- **Word (`.docx`) and Markdown (`.md`) — full round-trip.** In, edited, and back out as a new version of the same file.
- **PowerPoint (`.pptx`) and PDF — imported and governed, but not exported as a binary.** Their content comes in and becomes governed, and the **original file is kept as-is, as a pointer** — so nothing is lost. What this release does *not* do is rebuild a `.pptx` or `.pdf` binary on the way out. That's a deliberate, disclosed boundary, not a silent gap.

**Markdown is a content copy, not a conversion.** When a `.md` file comes in, Tropo gives you a consistent in-vault surface to edit its content — it does **not** convert your Markdown into some other format and hand it back changed. The bytes you wrote are the bytes it works with.

## The receipt — and why you don't have to trust us

Every round-trip produces a **receipt**, and the receipt is built to be checked by *you*, not just asserted by Tropo. It makes three promises:

- **Your content is byte-faithful.** The receipt compares the content extracted from your *real original file* against the content extracted from the *real exported file* — two genuinely different artifacts at two ends of the trip. It compares the words you wrote, not the container: a `.docx` is repackaged on the way out, so the raw bytes always differ, but your *content* is what must survive, and that's what the receipt checks.
- **Your content is kept separate from Tropo's chrome.** Frontmatter, provenance markers, and formatting that Tropo adds are excluded from the comparison — so a governed round-trip doesn't falsely cry "changed" over machinery you didn't write.
- **Every drop is disclosed.** If anything *was* dropped or couldn't be carried across, the receipt names it. A drop that happened but went unreported would be a silent lie — so the receipt reports what actually happened, including the uncomfortable parts.

The load-bearing word is **verifiable**. The hashes on the receipt are hashes of real files you hold — your original and your export — so you can recompute the verdict yourself with a standard checksum tool, without re-running Tropo or taking its word for anything.

## What this means for you

- **"How do I get my Word doc in and back out?"** Drop it in `04-external-work/`, edit it, export it — a new version lands back in that same folder.
- **"Will my content get mangled?"** No. The receipt proves your content is byte-faithful and lists anything that was dropped — and you can verify it yourself.
- **"Can I round-trip a PowerPoint the same way?"** Not in this release. PowerPoint and PDF import and become governed, but the original binary is kept as a pointer; there's no binary export back out yet.
- **"Is my Markdown converted into something else?"** No. It's a content-copy edit surface, not a format conversion.

Your work comes in whole, lives under your control while it's here, and leaves whole — with a receipt you can check. That's the boundary Tropo is built to be trusted across.
